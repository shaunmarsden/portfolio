When a retry is not enough.

Reliable Automation Lab

THE PROJECT

A fictional timesheet-to-finance integration, tested where the happy path ends.

Evidence & offline source →

Independent exercise.
Codex-assisted development.
No production deployment.

The problem

A transfer can succeed at its destination and still lose its confirmation. Retrying a basic script can post the same record twice. I wanted the comparison to expose that failure, not just demonstrate a successful transfer.

Architecture & approach

  1. Synthetic timesheets
  2. Validated, scoped worker
  3. Durable intent journal
  4. Idempotent local finance

Compare a basic script, a careful simulated manual checklist and a controlled Python/SQLite integration on the same frozen 24 scenarios. Controls include strict validation, scoped fictional identities, immutable keys, bounded retries, readback reconciliation and explicit exception dispositions. No LLM was needed.

What was actually tested

The original experiment ran three repetitions per design: 216 episodes across the same 24 authored scenarios. Later hardening added five actual child-process crash windows and 200 seeded recovery traces. The project has 47 automated tests.

A failure that changed the design

Confirmed replay initially concealed corruption: it could recreate a deleted finance row or replace a damaged receipt. Hardening routes confirmed intents to readback reconciliation; uncertain pending effects use idempotent recovery. A recurring mismatch also needed to reopen its exception.

Inspect the failure / control path +
  1. Fault injected

    Destination commits; confirmation is lost.

  2. Recovery boundary

    A durable pending intent retains the same destination key.

  3. Observed property

    The existing key is read back; recovery avoids a second posting.

Explanation of an executed fault-test path, not a live service or interactive finance action.

The limit of the result

The manual baseline is a simulation, with assumed effort. It also achieved 60/66 correct single postings and 6/6 recoveries. Five eligible records remained missing in both careful designs; corruption remained explicitly reported. These are local fictional systems, not distributed production infrastructure. Engineering cost, operator time and business ROI are unmeasured.

The lesson is not “automate everything”. Strong controls are justified when duplicate or uncertain effects matter and the destination supports reliable idempotency. A careful manual process remained a credible comparator.

Contribution & validation

Shaun commissioned the comparison and set the local-only, synthetic-data, evidence and approval boundaries. Implementation, scripted experiment execution, failure diagnosis and hardening were carried out with Codex. Automated tests establish artefact behaviour. Independent human validation was not performed.

What the work demonstrates

Integration design, recoverability, least privilege, non-AI comparison and the judgement to retain unresolved exceptions.

Workplace use would require representative authorised data, accountable owners, authenticated identities, security and operational review, and evidence of actual workflow value. None is claimed here.
